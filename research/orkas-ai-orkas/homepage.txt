Orkas is an open-source, local-first multi-agent AI desktop client for macOS and Windows. A super-powered Commander coordinates specialist agents to complete complex work together. What can I build with Orkas? Use Orkas as a coordinated AI team for complex work: research markets, turn findings into reports or slides, read PDFs with citations, fix bugs and open PRs, analyze data, create apps or videos, organize inbox and calendar work, connect external tools, and more, then turn repeated work into reusable agents and skills. How is Orkas different from ChatGPT, Codex, Claude Code, or Cline? ChatGPT is a hosted assistant, while Codex, Claude Code, and Cline focus on coding sessions. Orkas is an open-source, local-first AI workforce where a Commander coordinates specialist agents to complete complex work together. Do I need to bring my own model key? No. Orkas can provide managed official models that run through Orkas-managed model service. You can also connect your own providers through OAuth or API keys, including OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, Kimi, and GLM; bring-your-own-provider calls go directly to that provider. Will my files, history, or API keys be uploaded to Orkas? By default, your configs, history, knowledge bases, and generated files stay on your machine. If you bring your own keys, they are stored locally and requests go directly to your provider. Orkas-managed models and optional cloud features use Orkas services only for those selected features. Is Orkas free, and what would I pay for? The desktop app is free and includes 500 bonus credits for managed official models; bring-your-own-provider usage is billed by your provider. Free accounts also include limited project collaboration: 5 shared projects with up to 5 members each and 100 MB of cloud storage. Paid plans add monthly credits, multi-device sync, more cloud storage, and more shared projects and members. Remote control is planned. What are Orkas credits, and when do I need them? Credits meter the Orkas-managed model service only. If you connect your own provider key, those calls go straight to that provider and are billed by it, so they use no credits. Paid plans include a monthly allowance — 0–50,000 on Lite, 0–50,000 on Pro, and 0–50,000 on Max — and you can also buy lifetime credit packs as a one-time purchase, which work exactly like monthly credits and are available whether or not you subscribe (monthly credits are spent first). Consumption follows each provider's official pricing: roughly 100–300 credits per million input tokens, 2–6 per million cached input tokens, and 200–600 per million output tokens, so repeated context costs far less than it looks. Is Orkas open source, and where is the repository? Yes. The Orkas desktop client is open source under the MIT license at github.com/Orkas-AI/Orkas. You can read the source, build it yourself, and audit exactly what the app sends over the network. Can I self-host Orkas, and how does the open-source build differ from the official release? Yes. The open-source desktop edition is free to download and self-host, and it runs without an Orkas account: you add your own provider keys and your conversations, files, and keys stay on your machine. The official release adds the optional hosted services — managed models, multi-device sync, and cloud storage — and those need an Orkas account. One thing worth being clear about: Orkas is a desktop client, not a server, so self-hosting means running the app on your own machine rather than deploying a server for a team. Which platforms and interface languages does Orkas support? The official download page provides macOS and Windows builds. The open-source repository also supports Linux, which you build yourself. The interface offers 18 language options, including regional variants; you can switch language in the app at any time.
